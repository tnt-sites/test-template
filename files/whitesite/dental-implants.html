What are Dental Implants?

Dental implants are a modern, long-lasting way to replace missing teeth. Unlike bridges or dentures that rest on top of the gums, a titanium, biocompatible implant replaces the root itself. This small post is placed directly in the jawbone during a small surgical procedure, where it gradually fuses with the bone through a process called osseointegration. This creates a stable foundation for a custom-made crown (or sometimes another type of restoration, depending on the number of missing teeth) and also prevents the bone loss that often follows tooth loss.

Implants are versatile enough to replace a single tooth, several teeth, or even an entire arch. In some cases, patients missing all of their teeth can opt for All-on-X, which is a specialized form of dental implant placement that relies on just a few strategically placed posts that take advantage of the densest areas of the jaw.

Are You a Good Candidate for Dental Implants?

Most adults missing one or more teeth qualify for dental implant treatment. While individual assessment determines final eligibility, certain health factors strongly support successful outcomes.

Strong candidates typically have:

  • Healthy gums free from active periodontal disease
  • Sufficient jawbone density to anchor the titanium post
  • General health that supports minor oral surgery
  • Don’t smoke or use tobacco products, since tobacco significantly compromises healing and integration

Insufficient bone density from previous evaluations doesn't eliminate implant candidacy. Bone grafting procedures successfully rebuild the structural foundation needed for stable implant placement, often performed as a preparatory step.

Certain medical conditions like diabetes or specific medications warrant additional consideration but rarely disqualify patients entirely. Our doctors evaluate each patient's complete health profile to develop treatment approaches that maximize success and minimize complications.

What to Expect From Dental Implant Recovery

Proper preparation supports successful healing. Most patients experience mild soreness and swelling around the treatment site following implant placement. These symptoms are normal and typically resolve within a few days. Over-the-counter pain medications effectively manage any discomfort.

A soft food diet protects the surgical site during initial healing. Yogurt, scrambled eggs, soup, and mashed potatoes are excellent choices. Most patients resume work and normal activities within 24-48 hours, depending on their individual response.

You'll receive detailed post-operative instructions before leaving our office, including care protocols and warning signs to monitor. Our team monitors your progress at scheduled follow-up appointments to ensure proper integration and address any concerns promptly.

Benefits of Dental Implants

Dental implants don’t just allow you to replace your missing teeth; they also impart all kinds of unique advantages, including:

  • Dental implants can restore a majority of your original biting force thanks to the support your replacement teeth receive from the posts embedded in the jaw.
  • Dental implants prevent jawbone deterioration and shrinkage that occurs following tooth loss, which can affect your facial shape.
  • With the right care, they can last for decades or even a lifetime. They also don’t require any specialized maintenance or cleaning products, unlike traditional options.

Dental Implant Frequently Asked Questions

Do dental implants hurt?

Local anesthesia ensures comfort during placement. Most patients experience only mild soreness afterward, easily managed with over-the-counter pain relievers for a few days.

How long do dental implants last?

Research demonstrates success rates exceeding 95% over 10 years with proper oral hygiene and regular checkups. Many patients enjoy their implants for decades with appropriate maintenance.

Can I get a dental implant if I've had a tooth missing for years?

Time alone doesn't disqualify candidacy. Our team uses clinical examination and imaging to reveal bone density and tissue health, which determine whether immediate placement is possible or if preparatory treatment is needed.

What if I don't have enough bone for dental implants?

Bone grafting rebuilds the foundation needed for successful implant placement. This common procedure uses your body's natural healing ability to regenerate bone tissue where it has been lost.

Are dental implants covered by insurance?

Coverage varies widely between plans. We review your benefits upfront and explain exactly what your insurance covers versus your out-of-pocket investment before any treatment begins.

What's the difference between dental implants and dentures?

Implants fuse with your jawbone, creating permanent tooth replacement that feels and functions naturally. Dentures sit on gum tissue and can shift during eating or speaking, requiring adjustments as your mouth changes.
