What is TMJ Disorder?

Your TMJ (temporomandibular joints) connect your lower jaw to the rest of your skull, and you have one on each side of your face. They work with a group of muscles and ligaments to allow you to bite, chew, speak, yawn, and move your mouth. However, TMD occurs when these joints or the surrounding muscles become irritated, strained, or misaligned. Many people suffer from TMD without even realizing it, but those who do notice it often deal with uncomfortable and debilitating symptoms.

Common Symptoms of TMJ Disorder

TMJ disorder can show up in many ways, and symptoms often extend beyond the jaw itself. Common signs include jaw pain or tenderness, clicking or popping when you open and close your mouth, and limited jaw movement. Many people also experience frequent headaches, earaches, neck and shoulder tension, worn or chipped teeth (due to bruxism), or a bite that suddenly feels off. If any of these symptoms sound familiar, you shouldn’t wait to contact us to schedule a consultation so that we can begin discussing treatment options.

Our TMJ Treatment Options

Treating TMJ disorder requires tailoring the treatment to the cause and severity of your symptoms, meaning we have to start with an evaluation, assess your jaw and bite, and then go from there. One of the most common methods for relieving TMD involves occlusal splints. These are custom-made oral appliances worn over the teeth, typically at night. They hold the jaw in a more relaxed position and protect the teeth from unfavorable contact. Occlusal adjustments, also called equilibration, are also an option. This involves carefully and precisely reshaping certain teeth so that the bite is more harmonious and strain on the jaw joints is relieved. In some cases, BOTOX can also be used to help with TMD symptoms. During your consultation, we’ll review all of this, and our team is more than happy to answer any questions you have!
