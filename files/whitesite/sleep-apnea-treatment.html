The Dangers of Sleep Apnea

Sleep apnea is far more than just loud snoring. This condition causes your breathing to repeatedly pause or become shallow while you’re sleeping, and it can occur hundreds of times in a given night without you ever even realizing it! Left untreated, sleep apnea can lead to serious health problems including high blood pressure, heart disease, stroke, type 2 diabetes, and more. Many people also suffer from chronic daytime fatigue, morning headaches, trouble concentrating, and mood changes and irritability. Because sleep apnea affects both your overall health and your quality of life, early diagnosis is very important. If you or a loved one snore heavily, gasps for air at night, or wakes up tired no matter how long you sleep, it might be time for an evaluation.

Is an Oral Appliance Right For You?

Determining candidacy ensures optimal treatment outcomes. Oral appliance therapy is most effective for patients who meet specific clinical criteria.

Ideal candidates include those who:

  • Have been diagnosed with mild to moderate obstructive sleep apnea through a formal sleep study
  • Experience CPAP intolerance due to mask discomfort, equipment noise, or compliance difficulties
  • Present with chronic snoring requiring professional airway evaluation

A current sleep study is required before initiating treatment. Patients without recent diagnostic testing will receive appropriate referrals to ensure treatment decisions are based on clinical evidence and established protocols.

Sleep Dentistry Frequently Asked Questions

How does oral appliance effectiveness compare to CPAP therapy?

Clinical studies demonstrate that oral appliances provide effective treatment for mild to moderate obstructive sleep apnea. Treatment success depends on consistent nightly use, which research shows is higher with oral appliances compared to CPAP devices due to improved patient tolerance and comfort.

Is a sleep study required before treatment?

Yes. A formal sleep apnea diagnosis from a physician is required before appliance fabrication. This ensures treatment is appropriate for your specific condition. We coordinate with sleep specialists when a current sleep study is not available.

What insurance coverage applies to oral appliance therapy?

Medical insurance typically covers oral appliance therapy for diagnosed sleep apnea, as this falls under medical benefits rather than dental coverage. We verify benefits and handle pre-authorization with your insurance provider to clarify coverage before treatment begins.

What maintenance does the oral appliance for sleep apnea require?

Daily cleaning with a soft toothbrush and mild soap maintains appliance hygiene and function. We provide comprehensive care instructions during your fitting appointment, including specific cleaning protocols and maintenance schedules for your device.

What should I expect during the initial adjustment period with my oral appliance?

Initial jaw awareness or mild tenderness during the first two weeks is a normal adaptation response. We schedule follow-up appointments to monitor your adjustment and make precise modifications to optimize both comfort and therapeutic effectiveness.
