Root canals are a dental procedure used to repair and save teeth that have been damaged by decay or infection. Root canal treatment is often the best option to restore your tooth and prevent further problems from developing. In this blog, you’ll need to know everything about root canals – what it is, why it’s done, the procedure itself, potential risks, and frequently asked questions.

Root canal treatment is a type of dental procedure used to treat serious decay and infection in teeth. Root canals remove damaged or infected tissue from the root area of your tooth, preventing further damage and restoring it as close to its natural state as possible. Root canals can be a great option to preserve the health of your teeth when other treatments aren’t possible.

## What is Root Canal Treatment?

Root canal treatment involves removing the infected or damaged tissue from within the root area of your tooth, as well as any bacteria or debris that may have built up in there. After the removal process is complete, the root canal is sealed to prevent further damage or infection. Root canals are often used when traditional treatments such as fillings and crowns aren’t enough to restore your tooth.

## Why Root Canal Treatment?

Root canal treatment may be necessary if you have a large cavity that has gone untreated, an abscessed tooth, or a tooth that has been injured. Root canal treatment can be used to treat infection and decay, as well as prevent further damage from occurring. Root canals can also be used to remove teeth that have become severely damaged due to trauma or disease.

The Procedure: Root canal treatment is typically performed over the course of two visits to your dentist. During the first visit, your dentist will take x-rays to determine the extent of the damage and make a treatment plan. They may also administer medications or numbing agents to help reduce discomfort during the procedure. On the second visit, your dentist will perform the root canal procedure itself, removing any infected tissue and sealing it off with a material called gutta-percha.

Potential Risks: [Root canals](/dental-services/general-dentistry/root-canal-therapy/) are generally considered safe procedures, but there are some potential risks to be aware of. These include pain or discomfort during the procedure, infection, and swelling. There is also a risk that the root canal may not be successful, in which case you may require additional treatment such as an extraction.

### FAQs:

Q1: Is Root Canal Treatment painful?

A1: Root canal treatment itself is not usually painful, as your dentist will use numbing agents and medications to reduce discomfort. You may experience some mild pain or sensitivity after the procedure, but this should subside in a few days.

Q2: How long does Root Canal Treatment take?

A2: Root canal treatment typically takes two visits to your dentist, with each visit lasting about an hour. In some cases, the procedure may be completed in a single visit.

Q3: Is Root Canal Treatment covered by insurance?

A3: Most insurance plans will cover Root Canal Treatment, but check with your provider to confirm your coverage.

[Root canal treatment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_canal_treatment) can be a great option to preserve the health of your tooth when other treatments aren’t possible. It’s generally considered safe and there are few potential risks associated with the procedure. Root canal treatment can help to relieve pain and restore your tooth to its natural state.
