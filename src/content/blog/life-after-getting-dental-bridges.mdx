Dental bridges are a great approach to our lost teeth and dull appearance. They improve your ability to eat and smile while refilling the natural equilibrium of your teeth. You just got your dental bridge but now wondering what’s next? or how my life would be after this? Well, receiving a dental bridge is only the first step.

What comes next is a rigorous care regimen post-treatment that works hard to make sure your dental bridge works functional, stays comfortable and is long-lasting. Let's review everything in today’s blog from learning how to keep them in good condition to knowing which foods to avoid and more!

## Why Proper Care Matters For Dental Bridges?

If you fail to maintain dental bridges, it can lead to:

- Gum disease or cavity in the supporting teeth.
- Compromise with the durability of the bridge.
- Discomfort or deterioration of the bridge over time.

[Dental bridges](/dental-services/cosmetic-dentistry/dental-bridges/) can last 10–15 years or longer if it gets proper attention.

### Practice Good Oral Hygiene

Good oral hygiene is essential to maintain the health of the surrounding teeth and gums and the lifespan of your dental bridge. Use a soft-bristle toothbrush to brush and do the brushing at least 2 times a day, that’s very important. Using fluoride toothpaste is advisable to make the supporting teeth stronger. Don’t forget to keep the space beneath the bridge clean to avoid plaque accumulation.

### Floss Daily

[Flossing](https://www.colgate.com/en-us/oral-health/brushing-and-flossing/flossing-how-to) is essential, especially when it comes to cleaning under and around your dental bridge. Use interdental brushes or a floss threader a floss threader or interdental brushes to apply in difficult-to-reach places. It’d be better if you consider using a water flosser for complete cleaning and convenience.

### Pay Attention To Your Diet

Your dental bridge may get damaged or come loose due to certain foods.

- **Stay Away From:** Ice, hard nuts, and sticky candy.
- **Choose:** A well-balanced diet that includes lean proteins and prepared vegetables, which are soft and non-sticky.
- **Minimize Sugar Intake:** to reduce the risk of decay around the supporting teeth.

### Plan Scheduled Dental Visits

Routine examinations are essential for maintaining dental bridges.

- Dental bridge maintenance requires dental checkups.
- Our dentist is able to evaluate the bridge's state and make sure there are no hidden problems.

### Guard Against Grinding Your Teeth

Teeth grinding, or bruxism can put stress on both your natural teeth and dental bridge.

- If you grind your teeth at sleep, wear a nightguard.
- Deal with the stress-related behaviors that lead to bruxism.

### Do Not Use Teeth as Tools

Biting into hard things or opening items with your teeth might harm both your natural teeth and your dental bridge.

## Common Mistakes To Not Make

- **Ignoring Flossing:** Neglecting to floss under and around the bridge can lead to plaque buildup and gum disease.
- **Skipping Dental Visits:** Delaying dental visits might make small problems worse into more serious ones.
- **Ignoring Discomfort:** Get in touch with our dentist right away if your dental bridge feels loose or uncomfortable.

Taking care of your dental bridge isn’t complicated but requires consistent effort. Practicing good oral hygiene, making mindful dietary choices, and staying proactive with dental visits, can extend the life of your dental bridge. Remember, your dental bridge is an investment in your oral health—treat it with care!
