The strength and integrity of your [dental implant](/) crown is not guaranteed. A simple accident, combined with years of wear and tear, are detrimental to your teeth. Here are a few steps that you need to take if your crown implant breaks.

## Examine the Extent of Damage

It's common for some broken pieces of crown to cut into your gums or mouth. Review the extent of damage around your teeth. Then, look over the extent of damage on your broken crown. Know how serious the problem is or could become if you don't seek help right away.

- Collect the Broken Pieces
- Avoid discarding the broken pieces immediately. Save the pieces in case your dentist can repair the breakage.
- Contact a Dentist As Soon As Possible

Do not hesitate to contact your dentist as soon as possible to help replace a broken crown on an implant. A broken crown makes your teeth look crooked and is unsightly to look at, according to before-and-after pictures provided by [Austin Preventive Dentistry](/crowns-and-bridges/) providers. If the damage reaches your gums, it could become infected by bacteria. [Your crown needs to be replaced](https://www.webmd.com/oral-health/guide/repairing-a-chipped-or-broken-tooth#1) as soon as possible.

### Consider Your Replacement Options

If a crown breaks, you want to prevent this problem from occurring again. Consider [other types of crowns](https://www.healthline.com/find-care/articles/dentists/dental-crown) to use for a replacement. There are naturally colored crowns made of porcelain or ceramic along with ones made of metal like gold or silver. Porcelain-fused-to-metal (PFM) crowns have porcelain coverings with metal bases for added strength.

The result is the most natural-looking tooth replacement with a strong attachment. A crown is a visually aesthetic work of art, according to Austin cosmetic dentistry experts. Zirconia crowns are some of the most impressive features in dentistry.

These crowns are made of Zirconium, which is stronger and longer lasting than porcelain. The material is shiny and translucent, making it appealing in the light.

### Create a Prevention Plan

Prevent this incident of your crown breaking from occurring again. First, determine how the breakage occurred, whether it was caused by a slip and fall, the strong impact with an object or poor quality dental material.
