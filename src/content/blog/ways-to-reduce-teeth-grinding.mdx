Teeth grinding, also known as bruxism, mostly occurs at night. This is sleep or nocturnal bruxism, but it can also happen during the day. According to research, bruxism, 8% of adults experience chronic teeth grinding and clenching, whereas 50% of children are affected. When you grind your teeth subconsciously while you’re awake, it’s known as awake bruxism.  
Luckily, if you have this problem, there are certain remedies you can use to stop it. Some remedies are better than others, but the best option for ending bruxism is to seek help from an experienced dentist.

## Side Effects of Teeth Grinding and Clenching?

Teeth grinding is an annoying habit, even to the people around you. What’s worse is that without proper treatment, chronic bruxism and clenching predisposes you to developing certain health problems such as:

• Gum recession  
• Tooth cracks and chips  
• Fractured teeth  
• Tooth decay  
• Sleep problems  
• Jaw pain and headaches  
• Uneven tooth surfaces  
• Tooth loss  
• Periodontal disease

In serious cases, you might experience problems with speaking, chewing and swallowing. If left untreated, you will suffer from long-term complications like chronic ear pain, facial muscle enlargement and temporal mandibular joint disorders.

## When Should you Seek Help?

If you have a teeth grinding problem or suspect that it’s the cause of other symptoms or pain, you should see a dentist near you. The dentist will examine your teeth for signs of wear, to determine if you grind them and how severe it is. They will also look at your teeth alignment. Depending on the severity, the dentist will recommend certain treatments to help you.
