## What is IV Sedation Dentistry?

IV Sedation Dentistry is a type of dentistry that uses an intravenous (IV) injection to calm and sedate patients before dental procedures. This method allows dental professionals to perform more complex procedures with less anxiety or stress on the patient. It can also help reduce pain and discomfort during dental work.

## Types of IV Sedation Dentistry

There are a few different types of IV sedation dentistry, each with its benefits and drawbacks.

**1. General anesthesia** is the most common kind of IV sedation used in dentistry. It causes deep sleep and does not allow the patient to feel pain or discomfort. However, general anesthesia has many risks, including the risk of severe complications during surgery.

**2. Sedative-analgesic drugs** are another common type of IV sedation used in dentistry. These drugs relax the patient but do not cause unconsciousness or any markers of intoxication, such as slurred speech or excessive drooling. However, these drugs can also have side effects, such as drowsiness and dizziness, which may limit the extent to which the patient can be safely and comfortably sedated for dental work.

**3. Local anesthetics** are another type of IV sedation that is becoming increasingly popular in dentistry. These drugs numb only specific areas of the body, such as the dentist's hands, when performing delicate procedures like tooth removal or fillings without causing general anesthesia. Unlike general anesthesia and other types of IV sedation, local anesthetics do not put the patient.

## How Does IV Sedation Dentistry Works?

Generally, an IV is placed in a patient's arm, and anesthesia is administered through the IV. The patient then falls asleep, and the dentist can work on their teeth without them being aware. This type of dentistry is often used for challenging dental procedures for nervous patients, such as surgery or fillings.

## When is IV Sedation Dentistry the Right Choice for Me?

[IV sedation dentistry](/dental-services/sedation-dentistry/iv-sedation-dentistry/) is a safe and fair way to reduce anxiety and promote patient relaxation during dental procedures. In addition, patients generally experience less pain, increased comfort, and improved oral health outcomes when receiving IV sedation dentistry. Several types of IV sedation are available, each with unique benefits and drawbacks.

One common type of IV sedation is general anesthesia. General anesthesia typically produces profound sleepiness and unconsciousness, which makes it ideal for dental procedures that involve extensive surgery or exposure to potential danger. However, general anesthesia can also be dangerous if not used correctly. Side effects of general anesthesia include:

- Impaired breathing and circulation.
- Rapid heart rate.
- Changes in blood pressure.
- Even death.

Another type of IV sedation is regional anesthesia. Regional anesthesia involves numbing a specific body area with an anesthetic agent (usually lidocaine). This type of sedation is perfect for patients who experience mild to moderate anxiety during dental procedures. Regional anesthesia usually lasts about 30 minutes and does not produce the same deep sleep level as general anesthesia.

Some neuromuscular blocking agents (NMBAs) are short-acting anesthetics used most commonly during dental surgery. NMBAs block nerve impulses from reaching the muscles, which allows the dentist to perform delicate procedures without fear of causing injury to the patient's teeth or gums. NMBAs typically last about 45 minutes and are most effective.

## Tips for Preparing For Your dental appointment with IV Sedation Dentistry

If you are considering dental anesthesia with intravenous sedation, you should do a few things before your appointment.

1. Ensure you have all the necessary paperwork completed, including a health history and insurance information.

2. Arrive at the dental office at least two hours before your appointment to allow for adequate rest.

3. Avoid eating or drinking anything for several hours before your appointment, as this could decrease the effectiveness of the sedation medication.

4. Bring a copy of your health history and insurance information so our team can ensure you are appropriately covered during your procedure.

5. Consider sedation dentistry medications that can cause [drowsiness and dizziness](http://www.differencebetween.net/science/health/difference-between-dizziness-and-drowsiness/), so be careful when getting up from your chair or standing up after being sedated.

6. Our team will provide detailed instructions before your appointment about how to prepare for anesthesia and what to expect during the procedure.
