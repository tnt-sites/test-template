As teeth naturally age and weaken over time, many patients require dentures to replace missing teeth, and a dentist may recommend [implant supported dentures](/vista-ca/implant-supported-dentures/) for patients with considerable gaps. If a patient is missing many teeth, these can be an ideal option. However, not everyone is a candidate for them. Understanding the procedure for these specialized dentures can help determine if they are right for you.

## Understanding implant supported dentures

Unlike regular dentures, these dentures are secured in a patient’s jawbone. This prevents the dentures from coming loose and falling out, thus providing each patient with a sense of security and ease during everyday activities. Here is a breakdown of eligibility, installation procedure and care.

### Potential candidates

Almost anyone with broken or missing teeth is eligible for traditional dentures, but patients looking into implant supported ones must pass certain qualifications to get them. First, a patient’s bone density must be high, as the implants will be supported by the jawbone itself. Fortunately, a bone graft may be an option for creating a stable anchor for the implants. Second, since this is a form of surgery, patients must be relatively healthy with a good immune system and excellent gum condition. Patients that do not posses superior health may be at higher risk for receiving implants, and a dentist may suggest another course of action.

### Procedure

Getting **implant supported dentures** is a relatively straightforward step-by-step process. A temporary denture may be created for a patient to use throughout the process, which can take up to six months to complete. After the initial consultation, each implant is placed into the jawbone. The patient must then wait several months for the bone and implants to fuse together, creating a solid base for the dentures. Another surgery will expose the tops of the implants so that the dentist can attach the dentures to them. Once it has been confirmed that the dentures fit comfortably, they will be permanently adhered to the implants.

### Care

Properly caring for dentures is no different than a regular oral hygiene routine. Brushing the dentures twice a day and flossing can ensure the dentures remain in top condition. Make follow-up appointments with a dentist to check up on oral health and to get dentures cleaned.

## Differences between implant supported and traditional dentures

The main difference between the two types is how they are supported in the mouth. Traditional dentures are molded to the gums in order to sit comfortably on top of them. _Implant supported dentures_ are secured to the bone and can only be removed by the dentist. As a result, conventional dentures tend to rub and create sore spots on the gums, while implants stimulate the bone and may transform the shape of the mouth.

## Conclusion

Both types of dentures have advantages, but the choice ultimately comes down to what is better for each individual. Consult with a dentist to get a greater understanding of the differences and if implant supported dentures could be beneficial.
