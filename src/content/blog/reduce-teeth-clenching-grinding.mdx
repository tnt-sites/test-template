Teeth grinding, also known as bruxism, mostly occurs at night. This is sleep or nocturnal bruxism, but it can also happen during the day.

According to [research on bruxism](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/bruxism), 8% of adults experience chronic teeth clenching and grinding, whereas 50% of children are affected.

When you grind your teeth subconsciously while you’re awake, it’s known as awake bruxism.

Luckily, if you have this problem, there are certain remedies you can use to stop it.

Some remedies are better than others, but the best option for ending bruxism is to **seek help** from an [experienced cosmetic dentist](/austin-cosmetic-dentist/).

## Side Effects of Teeth Clenching and Grinding?

Teeth grinding is an annoying habit, even to the people around you. What’s worse is that without proper treatment, chronic [bruxism](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/190180.php) and clenching predispose you to develop certain health problems such as:

- Gum recession
- Tooth cracks and chips
- Fractured teeth
- Tooth decay
- Sleep problems
- Jaw pain and headaches
- Uneven tooth surfaces
- Tooth loss
- Periodontal disease

In serious cases, you might experience problems with speaking, chewing, and swallowing. If left untreated, you will suffer from long-term complications like chronic ear pain, facial muscle enlargement, and temporal mandibular joint disorders.

## When Should you Seek Help?

If you have a teeth-grinding problem or suspect that it’s the cause of other symptoms or pain, you should consult a cosmetic dentist nearby. Your dentist will examine your teeth for signs of wear, to determine if you grind them and how severe it is.

They will also look at your teeth' alignment. Depending on the severity, the dentist will recommend certain [treatments](https://www.healthline.com/health/how-to-stop-grinding-teeth#reduce-stress) to help you.
