## Painless cracked Tooth

No one wants to see a broken-off piece of [tooth](/) after eating. If there's no way to prevent a crack from forming, there are[ways to prevent it](/) from getting worse. The reality is that it could worsen over time. It's never a good idea to ignore a cracked tooth, and these are the reasons why.

### 1. Possibility for Infection

[A cracked tooth](https://www.healthline.com/health/cracked-tooth) is the perfect harbor for bacteria. When you brush a cracked tooth, it's difficult for the bristles to reach inside of the crack. If the crack is deep inside, the bacteria could reach the root and cause severe damage to the tooth, including nerve pain.

Whether the crack is superficial or deep, it's always ideal to have it repaired as soon as possible.

### 2. Possibility for Pain

Once a tooth is infected, [serious pain will follow](https://www.webmd.com/oral-health/toothache#1) if nothing is done to repair the damage right away. Some people complain of pain whenever they drink cold liquids or attempt to brush their teeth.

Other people have occasional bouts of unbearable pain that last for hours. They may find it difficult or impossible to carry on their daily activities.

Part of good [Austin Restorative Dentistry](/restorative-dentistry/) care is preventing the infection from happening. You can avoid the infection in the first place and prevent any further discomforts with the help of Toothcare providers.

### 3. A Cosmetic Flaw

A cracked tooth, even if it doesn't hurt, is unsightly to look at. Even if it's located at the back of the jaw, an uneven tooth is a nuisance. Some people are bothered simply by knowing that a damaged tooth exists in their mouths.

They may be candidates for Austin cosmetic dentistry services, such as crowns or veneers, that cover up imperfections.

### 4. Potential for Worsening Damage

It's possible for cracks in teeth to get longer or wider. Repeatedly placing pressure on the crack, by eating hard foods or brushing with excessive force, could make a small problem worse. A crack can get larger and lead to a bacterial infection or gum inflammation.
