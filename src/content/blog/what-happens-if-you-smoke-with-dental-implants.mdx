## Understanding The Impact That Smoking Has On Your Oral Health

When people smoke, it's virtually impossible for these individuals to maintain a normal pH at the mouth interior. Tobacco use raises the acidity of the mouth in a way that can be very [detrimental to the natural tooth structures](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4860921/) and their protective layer of enamel. In fact, smoking affects a person's oral health in a very vast range of ways. To start, it contributes to dehydration and dry mouth. This creates the perfect conditions for harmful, odor and decay-causing bacteria to proliferation. Smoking also constricts the blood vessels and limits the flow of blood to the mouth. Without an ample supply of nutrients and oxygen, the tooth structures become more vulnerable to damage and decay. In instances in which oral surgery such as a dental implant procedure is required, smoking additionally slows the healing process. It also undermines the integrity of bone structures throughout the body, especially the bone that supports the natural teeth. As such, among smokers, the failure rate for implants can be [as high as 20 percent](https://www.healthline.com/health/dental-implant-problems#success-factors).

Even before considering dental implants, patients at The Toothbar are advised to quit smoking. When seeking dental implants from a cosmetic dentist downtown, many people will find that smoking cessation is an essential step towards becoming a desirable candidate. For dental implants to be stable, they must be successfully grafted into the supporting bone. With the reduced blood flow and oxygen delivery that's caused by smoking, and with increases in harmful mouth bacteria, higher levels of acidity, and other side effects, this healing rarely occurs successfully in those who engage in continued tobacco use. When visiting a cosmetic dentist downtown Austin TX residents who smoke should inquire about their options in smoking cessation. For smokers, this is one of the best ways to [ensure the success of any dental treatment](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3894084/).
