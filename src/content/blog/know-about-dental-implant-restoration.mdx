### In dentistry, what is called dental implant restoration?

[Dental implant restoration is a procedure](https://www.healthline.com/health/dental-and-oral-health/dental-implant-procedure) that makes an artificial tooth replace one that has been lost. As the procedure moves forward, the implanted root will be connected to the tooth. It is put on top of the titanium post that serves as the tooth's root. The crown or fake tooth is then put on top of it. Some of the procedures used to restore dental implants with a crown

To replace a missing tooth, there are two ways to do it: with dental implants or with a dental bridge. These ways of restoring implants are screw-retained or cemented, and they work well. There are some advantages to the screw-retained implant restoration method over the cemented implant restoration method. For example, screw-retained implant restoration is easier to fix if a crown is broken.

### A two-step process of dental implant restoration

In most cases, the implant restoration process is a two-step process, but it could also be three steps. Because of many reasons, the patient may want to keep the process going for a while. People and the number of implants in their mouths affect how long it takes to restore their teeth with implants. A single tooth implant takes at least one or two hours to finish.

In the beginning, the surgeon makes sure that the bone is ready to accept the implant. The more implants put in, the more preparation will be done. The first important step is to put the implant in. The most crucial part of putting the implant in the bone is to make sure there is enough bone to hold the implant.

An implant that looks like a root is meant to be the root of a tooth. It is put where the missing tooth used to be by the surgeon. These titanium implants can be used in either the upper or lower jawbone, and they can be made to look like teeth.

Making the implant work takes between three and six months for it to become part of the bone. First, the implants are put in place, and then they start to fuse with the bone. Then, a second surgery is needed to ensure they stay in place. It usually takes four to six months for a lower jaw to heal so that implant restoration can start, but this can vary. The upper jaw may need up to a year to heal before the second surgery. X-rays show this.
