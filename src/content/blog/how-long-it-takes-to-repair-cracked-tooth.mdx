It can be extremely painful to have a cracked tooth, like literally and symbolically. Getting it fixed as soon as possible is essential to prevent more harm or discomfort, regardless of how small the chip is or how deep the fracture is. However, what is the actual duration of this process? The type of treatment required and the extent of the crack will determine the answer.

Your timetable can range from simple bonding operations to more involved options like root canals or crowns. We explain the various treatment options, their schedules, and what to anticipate during your recuperation in this guide. Find out how quickly you can resume smiling with confidence by reading on!

## Timeline for Repairing a Cracked Tooth: What Involves?

### Bonding of the teeth (30–60 minutes)

Dental bonding is a simple and efficient way to fix small fissures and is often used for [cracked tooth repair](/dental-services/general-dentistry/cracked-tooth-repair/). In this treatment, the dentist uses a special light to solidify a tooth-colored resin. Duration of this treatment is 30 to 60 minutes for a single visit. After the surgery, there is no downtime. You’re free to eat and drink as usual.

### Crown or dental filling (1-2 hours with a follow-up appointment)

A crown or filling can be required if the crack is more extensive. It’s inserted after the affected area is removed. While some dentists provide crowns on the same day, others need a follow-up appointment. Preparation time is one to two hours, with a follow-up appointment if necessary.

### Root Canal Treatment (60-90 minutes for each, 1-2 visits)

This treatment is required for a deep crack that affects the pulp.

The tooth is sealed after the infected pulp is extracted. It takes 60 to 90 minutes per appointment; two visits may be necessary in certain circumstances.

With a 95% success rate, root canals can keep natural teeth intact for many years, according to the [American Association of Endodontists](https://www.aae.org/)!

### Extracting and replacing a tooth (few weeks to few months)

Extraction can be the sole choice if the crack is too big. After the injured tooth is extracted, a bridge or implant is considered as a possible replacement. It takes 30 to 60 minutes for extraction, and a few weeks for the implant or bridge to fully heal and be placed.

While receiving an implant may take months, healing from removal takes roughly one to two weeks.

## Facts Influence The Treatment Timing

Some variables decide how long it takes to repair a broken tooth:

- More intricate techniques are needed for deeper cracks.
- While implants take longer, other procedures, like as bonding, are done quickly.
- In some cases, cutting-edge methods are applied to expedite the process, such as same-day crowns.
