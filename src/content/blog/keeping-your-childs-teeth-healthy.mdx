**Keeping Your Child's Teeth Healthy**

It is imperative to be pro-active in children's [dental care](/). Bad habits can set the backdrop to the start of dentin, enamel and gum disease. The care of a child's oral health starts at birth. Childhood is the opportune time to instill positive tooth care routines that can last a lifetime. Let's look at some questions parents may pose concerning Childhood dental care.

## **Should Baby's Teeth be Brush?**

The simple answer is yes. A baby can [begin to develop tooth decay](https://www.aapd.org/resources/parent/faq/) as soon as teeth begin to pierce the gum-line. In fact, [20% of children](https://www.cdc.gov/oralhealth/basics/childrens-oral-health/index.html) in the age range of 5 to 11 have tooth decay. It is urgent to begin caring for baby's oral health at birth if you do not want your child to be a statistic.

It is recommended to use a small, [soft-bristled toothbrush](https://www.webmd.com/parenting/baby/caring-babies-teeth#2) once a day on baby's tooth or use a soft rag twice a day to wipe down baby's gums. ## **How Can I Prevent Childhood Cavities?**

- Discuss a [fluoride varnish treatment with your dentist.](https://www.webmd.com/parenting/baby/caring-babies-teeth#2)
- Do not give them sugary drinks, juices or sodas.
- At bedtime, fill baby's bottle with water. Do not fill it with milk or juices.

*Sugar drinks promote teeth and gum disease.*
