[](https://www.mygreatsmile.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/shutterstock_1198321204-scaled.jpg)When it comes to replacing missing teeth, dental bridges and [implants](https://www.mygreatsmile.com/dental-implants.html) are two common options that you might consider. Both can restore dental function and aesthetics, but they differ in terms of procedure, cost, and longevity. Understanding the distinctions between dental bridges and implants can help you make informed decisions about tooth replacement. Discover the essential information you need to know below.

## Difference Between Dental Bridges and Implants

A dental bridge is a restoration that involves anchoring artificial teeth (pontics) between adjacent natural teeth. The bridge is typically made of porcelain and is cemented into place, effectively "bridging" the gap left by the missing tooth. In contrast, a dental implant substitutes a natural tooth root by surgically implanting a titanium post into the jaw. A crown is affixed to the implant, creating a permanent replacement tooth when the implant and bone have fused (this is known as [osseointegration](https://www.mygreatsmile.com/dental-implants.html)). 

## Is a Dental Bridge Cheaper Than an Implant?

In general, dental bridges tend to be more cost-effective upfront compared to dental implants. The process of placing a bridge is less complex and typically requires fewer dental appointments. So, it ultimately lowers the overall treatment costs. However, you need to consider the long-term costs and benefits of each option.  While bridges may be initially cheaper, they may require replacement or repair over time. Additionally, bridges do not stimulate the jawbone like implants do, which can lead to bone loss over time and potential changes in facial structure. Dental implants also offer a more permanent and durable solution for tooth replacement.
