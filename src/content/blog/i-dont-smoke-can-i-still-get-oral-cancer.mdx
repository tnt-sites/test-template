In the United States, oral cancer accounts for three percent of all cancer incidents. Although smoking is a significant risk factor in the development of this condition, you don't have to be an active smoker to be at risk.

Other risk factors include poor [dental hygiene](/), alcoholism, Human Papillomavirus (HPV) infection and suppressed immune system. Studies also show [high incidence in men](https://www.nidcr.nih.gov/research/data-statistics/oral-cancer/incidence#:~:text=Overall%2C%2010.5%20adults%20per%20100%2C000,cancer%20rates%20increase%20with%20age.) and increased risk with age.

## Poor oral hygiene can cause cancer

Recent research indicates an [increase in oral cancer deaths](https://oralcancerfoundation.org/facts/#:~:text=Rates%20of%20occurrence%20in%20the,hour%2C%2024%20hours%20per%20day.). This is not because the condition is hard to diagnose, but due to late discovery as most people don't pay attention to dental health or don't go for regular dental check-ups. The early stages of oral cancer may be unnoticeable, but many cases have been identified during routine dental exams. This is why you need to seek the services of a competent dentist.

A study investigating the [relationship between oral hygiene and oral cancer](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6414580/) found that individuals with poor oral practices were at a higher risk of developing the disease as it enhanced the carcinogenic potential of other risk factors other than smoking.

If oral cancer is not diagnosed and treated at early onset, it can cause severe damage to teeth, gums and the entire oral cavity. Even worse, this cancer may spread to other parts of the body, making treatment difficult and expensive.

## Basic oral care tips

- Brush your teeth with quality dental products by moving your brush in gentle, circular motions to get rid of plaque.
- Remember to brush your tongue because plaque build-up can lead to bad odor and other oral health issues.
- Floss your teeth frequently because flossing reduces plaque, [prevents inflammation](https://www.healthline.com/health/dental-and-oral-health/best-practices-for-healthy-teeth#Take-care-of-your-teeth) and enables you to examine swelling and redness in your mouth.
- Visit your Austin dentist at least once a year for[professional cleaning](/5-tips-for-good-oral-hygiene-and-healthy-smiles/) and oral examination.

Today, dentists are playing an important role in oral cancer diagnosis and prevention. Expect an oral cancer screening during a routine visit to your Austin Preventative Dentistry. The dentist will check for lumps or any abnormalities in your dental cavity and throat.

If any signs of oral cancer are noted, he or she may recommend follow-up visits to monitor progression. You may also require a biopsy to investigate the presence of any cancerous cells.
