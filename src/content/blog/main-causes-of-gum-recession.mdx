## Causes of Gum Recession

Gum recession is a condition in which the gum tissue around the teeth wears away, exposing the roots of the teeth. Recession can occur gradually or suddenly, affecting one tooth or multiple teeth.

Seeing a dentist as soon as possible is essential if you think you may be experiencing gum recession. Treatment options will vary depending on the severity of the condition. In some cases, simple teeth cleaning may be all that is necessary. More severe cases may require periodontal surgery or other dental procedures.

There are several reasons why gum recession may occur, including:

1. Over time, aggressive brushing can damage the gum tissue and cause it to recede.
2. Periodontal disease is an infection of the gums that can destroy the tissue and bone that support the teeth.
3. Grinding or clenching can put too much pressure on the teeth and gums, causing the tissue to recede.
4. Some people are more prone to recession in the gum due to genetics.
5. Brushing too hard or too aggressively.
6. Dental plaque or tartar buildup.
7. Trauma or injury to your gum tissue.
8. Abnormal tooth positioning (misalignment).
9. Smoking or chewing tobacco use.
10. Lip and tongue piercings.

### Effects of Gum Recession

**Physically**

When the roots of the teeth are exposed, they are more susceptible to temperature changes and can become sensitive to hot or cold food and drinks.  
The roots of the teeth are not as protected as the rest of the tooth, so they are more susceptible to decay  
The bone around the teeth can start to deteriorate without the support of the gum tissue.  
In severe cases, the teeth can become loose and fall out.

Emotionally, gum recession can cause a great deal of stress and anxiety. It can also affect your self-esteem and confidence. If you are embarrassed about your smile, you may avoid social situations or feel self-conscious when talking to others.

Financially, recession can be costly to treat. If left untreated, it can lead to tooth loss, requiring more extensive and expensive treatment.
