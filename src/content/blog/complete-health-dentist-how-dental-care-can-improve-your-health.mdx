A [complete health dentist](/about/) can help you improve your overall health. The human metabolism is a holistic system. It is made up of many interconnected functions. The American Dental Association and other medical bodies have long recognized a correlation between oral health and general health. They have called the mouth “the portal” through which many infections enter. Dental problems themselves deplete the immune system and strain the body’s health.

## The holistic human metabolism

Even a toothache has an impact on a person’s overall health, taxing their endurance. An abscess or gum infection may leak fluids and bacteria into the bloodstream. This spreads the infection further. Infections of the mouth can spread to the nearby nose, eyes and ears. Problems in the teeth and gums may also be symptoms, rather than causes, of illness elsewhere in the body. Looking after the teeth properly contributes to maintaining the health of the whole body.

### How organs affect one another

The eyes, ears, nose and throat are in close proximity, affecting one another. There are direct channels between the throat and the nose, ears and eyes, not to mention the sinuses. Problems in any one of these can often affect the others. Removing a person’s tonsils increases the risk of oral infections spreading down the throat. Inflammation, pain and discharge from any of these areas may be only symptoms of another problem nearby.

Infections of the gums, teeth and jaw can weaken the metabolism’s resistance to other infections. They can also migrate down the throat and through the bloodstream, infecting other organs. According to the American Dental Association (ADA), the oral health connection correlates to other health issues. The North Carolina School of Dentistry has found a correlation between gum disease and the incidence of heart problems.

### Definition of a healthy mouth

A complete health dentist looks for a baseline for oral health. The checklist includes the health of the gums and teeth and the daily habits of the person. Indicators of poor dental health include toothache, bleeding gums, bad breath and a buildup of plaque and tartar. A complete health dentist will also look for evident tooth decay, inflammation and recessed gums. In some people, grinding the teeth may cause damage, affecting oral health.

Daily brushing and flossing, as well as a good mouthwash, all help maintain a healthy mouth. Regular dental checkups can identify problems in the mouth early. This can help the dentist spot other health issues. Bad habits, especially smoking and high-sugar drinks, can erode the teeth and contribute to bad oral health. Aesthetics, the whiteness of the teeth and erosion from grinding can also contribute to the health assessment of a person’s mouth.
