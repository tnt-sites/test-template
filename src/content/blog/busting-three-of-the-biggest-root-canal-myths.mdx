One of the most feared dental treatments out there is [root canals](/root-canal-therapy/). They have such a bad reputation that many patients often push back on this beneficial service. You might wonder what has brought about this negative stigma, but it’s all because of myths you might’ve heard. Many of them aren’t true, so here is the truth behind three misconceptions you may have come across.

## Myth #1: Root Canals Are Very Painful to Get

Possibly the biggest myth people spread is that root canals cause more pain than they help. However, this isn’t true at all. Your dentist will numb the area around the tooth with powerful local anesthesia; that way, you don’t feel much discomfort, or have it be very minor.

You may also ask about [dental sedation](/dental-anxiety-and-fear/), which can help you feel more comfortable during your procedure. Those who have gotten this treatment often say it feels like getting a filling. That said, you will feel sore afterward, but you should be able to manage it with over-the-counter pain relievers.

## Myth #2: It’s Better to Have a Tooth Removed Than Get a Root Canal

Root canals are needed in the first place because of an infection inside a tooth. A few patients might think removing the structure will be best, but your dentist considers an extraction the last resort.

While modern replacements are reliable and long-lasting, they aren’t quite up to par with your home-grown grin. Your provider will work to save your tooth and restore it with another treatment like a crown. That way, you can continue getting good use of it and avoid any trouble that a gap in your smile can cause.

## Myth #3: You Have to Be in Pain to Get a Root Canal

An infected tooth often causes severe pain, but this discomfort doesn’t always mean you need a root canal. Your dentist might’ve caught the issue early enough before it became a big headache to deal with.

On the flip side, the bacteria might’ve damaged the nerve so much already that it can’t send out signals anymore. Regardless, it’s important you get this procedure to increase your chances of saving your smile; that way, you feel confident knowing it’s in great shape.

With all that said, you should know the facts when it comes to dental treatments like root canals. Try not to believe myths you’ve seen going around, so consider talking to your dentist if you have any concerns. That way, you feel your best when deciding about your oral health.
