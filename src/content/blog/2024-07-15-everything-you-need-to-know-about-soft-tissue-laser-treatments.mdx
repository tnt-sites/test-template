[Soft tissue laser treatments](https://www.mygreatsmile.com/gum-disease-treatment.html#laser) have revolutionized the field of dentistry by offering a minimally invasive and highly effective method for treating various oral health issues. Understanding laser dentistry and available treatments can help you make informed decisions about your dental care.

## What is Soft Tissue Laser Treatment?

Soft tissue laser treatments utilize focused light energy to precisely target and treat soft tissues in the mouth, such as gums and the lining of the cheeks. These lasers operate at different wavelengths, allowing them to cut, coagulate, and vaporize tissue with high accuracy. Dentists use soft tissue lasers for a variety of procedures, including gum reshaping, removing excess tissue, treating [periodontal disease](https://www.mygreatsmile.com/gum-disease-treatment.html#laser), and performing [biopsies](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diagnostics/15458-biopsy-overview). The laser's precision ensures minimal damage to surrounding tissues, promoting faster healing and reduced discomfort.

## The Benefits of Soft Tissue Laser Treatments

One of the primary advantages of soft tissue laser treatments is their precision. The focused light beam allows dentists to target specific areas without affecting the surrounding tissues. It can result in less trauma and a quicker recovery. This precision is particularly beneficial for procedures such as gum reshaping. Moreover, it can reduce pain and discomfort. The laser cauterizes as it cuts, reducing the need for sutures and minimizing bleeding. This results in less postoperative pain and swelling and can make the recovery process more comfortable. The laser's ability to minimize tissue damage and promote blood clotting enhances the healing process. Patients typically experience faster recovery times compared to traditional surgical methods. The reduced need for sutures and lower risk of infection further contribute to quicker healing. Lastly, the high-energy light beam of the laser sterilizes the treatment area, significantly reducing the risk of bacterial infection. This sterilization effect enhances the overall safety of the procedure and promotes better long-term outcomes.
