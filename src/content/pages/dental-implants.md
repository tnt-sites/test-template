- _component: page-sections/services/service-split
  heading: What are Dental Implants?
  anchorName: ''
  subtext: 'Dental implants are a modern, long-lasting way to replace missing teeth. Unlike bridges or
    dentures that rest on top of the gums, a titanium, biocompatible implant replaces the root itself.
    This small post is placed directly in the jawbone during a small surgical procedure, where it gradually
    fuses with the bone through a process called osseointegration. This creates a stable foundation for
    a custom-made crown (or sometimes another type of restoration, depending on the number of missing
    teeth) and also prevents the bone loss that often follows tooth loss.


    Implants are versatile enough to replace a single tooth, several teeth, or even an entire arch. In
    some cases, patients missing all of their teeth can opt for All-on-X, which is a specialized form
    of dental implant placement that relies on just a few strategically placed posts that take advantage
    of the densest areas of the jaw.'

  heading: Are You a Good Candidate for Dental Implants?
  anchorName: ''
  subtext: 'Most adults missing one or more teeth qualify for dental implant treatment. While individual
    assessment determines final eligibility, certain health factors strongly support successful outcomes.


    Strong candidates typically have:


    - Healthy gums free from active periodontal disease

    - Sufficient jawbone density to anchor the titanium post

    - General health that supports minor oral surgery

    - Don’t smoke or use tobacco products, since tobacco significantly compromises healing and integration


    Insufficient bone density from previous evaluations doesn''t eliminate implant candidacy. Bone grafting
    procedures successfully rebuild the structural foundation needed for stable implant placement, often
    performed as a preparatory step.


    Certain medical conditions like diabetes or specific medications warrant additional consideration
    but rarely disqualify patients entirely. Our doctors evaluate each patient''s complete health profile
