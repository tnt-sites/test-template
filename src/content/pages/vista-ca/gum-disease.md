    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/heading
      text: Causes of Gum Disease
      level: h2
      size: md
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: There are several reasons that gum disease develops. The primary causes are plaque and tartar.
        Anything that becomes stuck between your teeth, gums or under the gum tissue, can cause irritation
        and swelling. Plaque is a problem that everyone deals with; however, it is the buildup under the
        gums that really causes issues. There are other risk factors to consider like pregnancy, diabetes,
        and smoking, since they restrict blood flow to the gums, causing them to swell.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/heading
      text: Ways to Prevent Gum Disease
      level: h3
      size: md
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: We recommend doing what you can to prevent gum disease so you do not experience discomfort
        or additional health problems. One of the best ways to prevent this condition is to keep your
        teeth clean. At North County Cosmetic and Implant Dentistry, we encourage our Vista patients to
        brush their teeth two to three times a day and to do so in a circular pattern, moving food particles
        and plaque away from the gums toward the center of the mouth. You also need to floss on a daily
        basis, and be sure to reach all the way down, rather than stopping at your gum line. Using an
        antiseptic mouthwash can also help kill lingering bacteria.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: You also need to have your teeth cleaned at least twice per year. It is difficult to remove
        all of the plaque at home, and tartar is too hard to remove by brushing and flossing. We can remove
        both plaque and tartar in our dental office and reduce your risk of gum disease. Simultaneously,
        we recommend that you stop smoking, keep your blood sugar under control, and drink plenty of water,
        since this can also help improve your gum health.

    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/heading
      text: Treatment Options for Gum Disease
      level: h3
      size: md
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: 'There are several ways to treat gum disease, and what we recommend will be based on how far
        the condition has progressed. To start, we typically recommend that our patients have a standard
        teeth cleaning. If you are only at the beginning stages of the condition, that is likely all you
        will need to return your gums to good health. However, if the condition has progressed further,
        you may need a dentist to provide you with the following:'
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/list
      listType: bullet
      items:
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/list/list-item
        text: Periodontal infection therapy. This is a therapy where chemicals are used to slow the growth
          of gum disease. In this case, your dentist may prescribe the use of an antiseptic mouth rinse,
          designed to reduce or fight gum disease. Used daily, it can work to kill bacteria.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/list/list-item
        text: Deep cleaning. A deep cleaning is typically conducted in four appointments. Your mouth is
          broken up into quadrants, with each area being cleaned on a different day. In a deep cleaning,
          the gums need to be pulled away from the teeth so a dentist can scrape off the plaque and tartar
          underneath the gum tissue. Naturally, this procedure can cause discomfort, which is why it is
          completed over multiple visits. However, some dentists use lasers, which can expedite the process.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/list/list-item
        text: Gum grafts. If your gums have receded and too much of your teeth or roots are showing, a
          gum graft may be necessary to restore the gum tissue back around the teeth. This can be done
          by suturing tissue from the roof of your mouth to your gums or using tissue from a donor. In
          both cases, the gums will be secured back around your teeth, like they were prior to you developing
          gum disease.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/list/list-item
        text: Antibiotics. Very often, your Vista dentist will prescribe antibiotics to be taken in combination
          with gum therapy. This is done to eliminate any infection that could be lingering.

    contentSections:
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/heading
      text: Why You Could Need a Bone Graft Following Severe Gum Disease
      level: h2
      size: md
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: Severe gum disease can drastically and negatively impact your oral health. As gum disease
        progresses, your gums will swell and then begin to recede or pull away from your tooth structure.
        This is problematic because the more of your teeth and roots that are exposed, the more likely
        it is that bacteria will begin to attack them. Simultaneously, your jawbone can also be negatively
        impacted and begin to lose its density.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: If you do suffer from bone loss, you may need a bone grafting procedure. During a bone graft,
        we place synthetic bone in the area where you experienced loss to encourage bone growth. This
        can be a necessary procedure when you have experienced enough bone loss to negatively impact the
        health and stability of your other teeth or the appearance of your face. Whether or not this is
        necessary is highly dependent on what your X-rays show. However, if you identify signs of gum
        disease and treat it early, you are likely to prevent the need for this procedure.

    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/heading
      text: Definition of Gum Disease Terminology
      level: h3
      size: md
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list
      items:
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Chronic Periodontitis
        text: Chronic periodontitis is the most common form of periodontitis, involving inflammation of
          the tissue surrounding the teeth and pockets forming as well.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Necrotizing Periodontal Disease
        text: Necrotizing periodontal disease is an infection that causes lesions to form on the face
          and other symptoms due to the necrosis of gingival tissues, periodontal ligaments and alveolar
          bone.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Periapical Cyst
        text: A periapical cyst is a pathological cavity, roots of the teeth, that typically has epithelium
          in the lining and contains fluid or soft matter.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Periodontal Disease
        text: Periodontal disease is a serious inflammation of the gingival tissues and the periodontal
          membrane of the teeth that causes pain and helps form deeper gingival sulcus.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Periodontal Ligament
        text: Periodontal ligaments are a group of tissue fibers that help attach the tooth to the alveolar
          bone, which can sustain damage from gum disease or improper oral hygiene.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Periodontal Pocket
        text: A periodontal pocket is a potential area of space, known as a gingival sulcus, which is
          deeper than normal and can contain bacteria that cause an infection.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Periodontal Surgery
        text: Periodontal surgery is the treatment of extreme levels of periodontal disease that can involve
          multiple techniques to remove the inflamed tissue and infection before it spreads.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Scaling and Root Planing
        text: Scaling and root planing is a non-surgical therapy that involves the removal of dental plaque
          in hard to reach places with patients who do not take proper care of their teeth.
