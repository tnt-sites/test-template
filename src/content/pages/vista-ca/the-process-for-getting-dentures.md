    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: Following the loss of one's teeth, resorption will begin to take effect. Adult teeth stimulate
        the jawbone, sending signals throughout the body. Once teeth fall out and stop stimulating the
        jawbone, the body will absorb the calcium in the jawbone to distribute to other areas of the body.
        Due to this, many people will experience a change in facial structure after losing some or all
        of their teeth.

        with missing teeth, including:'
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/list

      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/list/list-item

      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/list/list-item
        text: Risk of gum disease
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/list/list-item

    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text

        risk contracting oral infections such as gum disease. Normally, plaque will catch between the
        teeth and spread infection if left untreated. Without teeth, people are still at a risk for acquiring
        an infection. The best option for many patients is to seek customized dentures from a professional.

  - _component: page-sections/builders/custom-section
    label: The Dentures Process
    contentSections:
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/heading
      text: The Dentures Process
      level: h2
      size: md
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: To begin the dentures process, we will need to examine the patient and determine the best
        choice from full dentures, partial dentures, or another option. In some cases, if the remaining
        teeth are infected or weak, we will recommend a tooth extraction first. By removing the remaining
        teeth, we can then provide a set of fully-functional artificial teeth by installing dentures.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: Once we determine the best course of action, we will clean the teeth and remove any, if necessary.
        We will then make a mold and take measurements of the mouth. We will use this mold to develop
        the dentures and ensure they are a custom fit to the individual patient. This way, patients will
        not have to spend as much time worrying about the dentures falling out or being uncomfortable.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: It is important to note that there will be an adjustment period for the patient when first
        wearing the dentures. Following the appointment, the patients may need to wear temporary dentures
        until the new ones are complete. However, this step in the process can vary per each situation.
        We will send the mold and measurements to a dental lab that will create the custom dentures.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: After we complete the dentures, we will have the patient come in for a fitting. During the
        fitting process, we will make sure that the dentures fit properly and make any necessary changes.
        We will also go over proper care for the dentures and recommend the time for the next appointment
        to keep an eye on the dentures. There are a few things that patients need to know about the adjustment
        period.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: It will take around two weeks to completely adjust to the dentures and using them. The first
        step will be to avoid eating hard or chewy foods such as nuts and caramel. Proper oral hygiene
        will involve removing the dentures at night and leaving them in a special solution overnight.
        The patient will still need to brush the gums and ensure that there are no bits of food in the
        dentures.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: During the first few weeks, there may be some minor irritation and discomfort. However, the
        discomfort will subside after the adjustment period. The patient may need more adjustments in
        the months ahead, which we will be happy to schedule when necessary.

  - _component: page-sections/builders/custom-section
    label: Definition of Denture Terminology
    contentSections:
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/heading
      text: Definition of Denture Terminology
      level: h3
      size: md
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list
      items:
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Alveolar Bone
        text: The alveolar bone is the bone surrounding the root of the tooth that keeps the tooth in
          place.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Clasp
        text: A clasp is a device that holds a removable partial denture prosthesis to the teeth.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Denture Base
        text: The denture base is the part of the denture that connects the artificial teeth with the
          soft tissue of the gums.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Edentulous
        text: Edentulous is a term that applies to people who do not have any teeth.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Periodontal Disease
        text: Periodontal disease is a condition that causes inflammation of the gingival tissues and
          membrane of the teeth, leading to tooth loss without professional treatment.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Pontic
        text: Pontic is another term for an artificial tooth on a fixed partial denture.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Rebase
        text: Rebase is the process of refitting denture prosthesis by replacing the base material.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Reline
        text: Reline is when a professional resurfaces the surface of the prosthesis with a new base material.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Resin/Acrylic
        text: Resin and Acrylic are resinous materials that can be components in a denture base.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Stomatitis
        text: Stomatitis is the inflammation of the tissue that is underlying a denture that does not
          fit properly. It can also result from other oral health factors.
