    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: During the process known as resorption, the body leaches calcium from the jawbone that no
        longer has teeth stimulating it. The body then distributes the calcium to other areas of the body.
        While natural tooth loss is a common part of aging, we can help prevent resorption with dentures.
        Since the jawbone will continue to recede over time, the dentures will need adjustments from a
        professional. Fortunately, altering the size of dentures is an easy procedure that we can complete
        in our office, known as relining.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: In simple terms, relining is the procedure in which we reshape the underside of the denture
        to make it more comfortable as it rests against the gums. There are two separate methods for relining,
        hard reline and soft reline, that we will use depending on the needs of the patient. During the
        relining process, we can complete this procedure in our office or send the dentures to a dental
        lab. With pros and cons to each, it is important to ask a professional for guidance on the most
        effective option.

  - _component: page-sections/builders/custom-section
    label: Soft reline
    contentSections:
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/heading
      text: Soft reline
      level: h2
      size: md
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: A soft reline involves the use of a liquid polymer that we layer into the denture to create
        depth and a cushion. This "cushion" will tend to be more comfortable than a hard reline and is
        a relatively quick procedure. In fact, patients can receive the soft reline chairside and avoid
        having to send the dentures to a lab and go several days with temporary dentures or nothing at
        all.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: However, a soft reline may not last as long as a hard reline and can require more frequent
        visits to continue keeping up with the receding jaw. For some people, this can result in more
        visits and higher costs that they may not be able to spend. We understand that dentures are crucial
        to one's lifestyle, including the ability to speak and eat. Thus, we will do everything we can
        to efficiently and properly repair the dentures.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/heading
      text: Hard reline
      level: h3
      size: md
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: A hard reline involves using material that is much more similar to the hard base of the denture.
        While a hard and soft reline accomplish the same goal, the hard reline will normally produce a
        more permanent result. While some professionals can complete the hard reline chairside, many recommend
        sending it to a dental lab to ensure that everything goes without a problem.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: However, a hard reline will take more time to complete since it is at a dental lab. In this
        case, we will go over possible replacement options and help to determine the best course of action
        for the patient's needs. Relining dentures is not a procedure that anyone can complete at home.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/heading
      text: Relining is essential for denture care
      level: h3
      size: md
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: Dentures can last anywhere from 5 to 10 years. However, without proper care, their lifespan
        will be less than what many prefer. The most effective method for getting the best mileage out
        of dentures is by following care instructions from the dental professional and scheduling regular
        appointments for denture care. We can use a denture relining to help patients keep the same pair
        of dentures they are comfortable with and prevent them from spending large amounts of money on
        new pairs.

    contentSections:
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/heading
      text: Definition of Denture Terminology
      level: h3
      size: md
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list
      items:
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Alveolar Bone
        text: The alveolar bone is the bone surrounding the root of the tooth that keeps the tooth in
          place.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Clasp
        text: A clasp is a device that holds a removable partial denture prosthesis to the teeth.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Denture Base
        text: The denture base is the part of the denture that connects the artificial teeth with the
          soft tissue of the gums.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Edentulous
        text: Edentulous is a term that applies to people who do not have any teeth.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Periodontal Disease
        text: Periodontal disease is a condition that causes inflammation of the gingival tissues and
          membrane of the teeth, leading to tooth loss without professional treatment.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Pontic
        text: Pontic is another term for an artificial tooth on a fixed partial denture.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Rebase
        text: Rebase is the process of refitting denture prosthesis by replacing the base material.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Reline
        text: Reline is when a professional resurfaces the surface of the prosthesis with a new base material.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Resin/Acrylic
        text: Resin and Acrylic are resinous materials that can be components in a denture base.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Stomatitis
        text: Stomatitis is the inflammation of the tissue that is underlying a denture that does not
          fit properly. It can also result from other oral health factors.
