    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: Another factor to compare when evaluating Clear Aligner Therapy vs. traditional braces for
        teens is oral hygiene. This is a big deal because, as a dentist, we do not want any of our patients
        to suffer from decay and as a parent, you do not want your child to end up with a bunch of cavities.
        At North County Cosmetic and Implant Dentistry, our solution is to provide teens with Clear Aligner
        Therapy instead of braces. When wearing braces, the risk for decay naturally goes up because it
        is harder to keep teeth clean. With Clear Aligner Therapy, the aligners come out while brushing
        so their oral hygiene routine can remain virtually the same. The only slight difference is that
        the aligners need to be brushed with warm water and a soft toothbrush before being put back in.
        We can also recommend a special cleaning solution but otherwise, the process is the same as always
        and completely easy. If your teen gets braces instead, they will need to use a special flossing
        tool and a brush that looks like a tree to get the food particles and plaque off of the area around
        the brackets. This has to be repeated for every tooth so you can imagine how much longer it will
        take.

    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/heading
      text: Definition of Clear Aligner Therapy Terminology
      level: h3
      size: md
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list
      items:
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Aligner Trays
        text: With Clear Aligner Therapy treatment, the patient will receive a series of aligner trays
          and swap out each one for the next one in the series every two weeks in order to gradually straighten
          the teeth.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Blue Compliance Indicator (Clear Aligner Therapy Teen)
        text: The blue compliance indicator is a small blue dot on the aligners that will fade from blue
          to clear in order to indicate if the patient is wearing the aligner for the proper amount of
          time and where the patient is in the Clear Aligner Therapy process.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: ClinCheck® Software
        text: ClinCheck® software allows professionals to map out the straightening process the patient’s
          teeth will go through with Clear Aligner Therapy in great detail.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Gum Line
        text: The gum line is the line in the mouth where the teeth and gums meet. If a patient struggles
          with gum recession, then the gum tissue around the teeth may begin to wear away.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Incisal Ridges
        text: Incisal ridges are the portion of the crown of the tooth that makes up the incisal portion
          and can be a direct cause of overbite if they are extended too far.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: iTero Element® Scanner
        text: An iTero Element® scanner allows professionals to create a 3D image of the patient’s teeth
          in minutes and predict what it will take to move the teeth into proper alignment.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Malocclusion
        text: Malocclusion is the condition in which the upper and lower teeth do not meet properly when
          the bite is closed. Conditions of malocclusion can include overbite, underbite, crossbite and
          open bite; all of which are treatable with Clear Aligner Therapy.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Overbite
        text: An overbite is a type of malocclusion that occurs when the upper teeth jut out over the
          lower teeth, covering them and causing other issues.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Polyurethane Resin
        text: Polyurethane Resin is a USP Class VI medical grade, high molecular weight compound that
          makes up the aligners.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: SmartForce Attachments
        text: SmartForce attachments are small attachments that professionals place on the patient’s teeth
          before placing aligners to help move the teeth.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Smart Track® Material
        text: Smart Track® material is in aligners to increase comfort, improve control of movement, increases
          the speed of treatment and applies a gentle force to the teeth over time.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Smile-Scan
        text: Smile-Scan analyzes the way a patient smiles by measuring the position of the mouth and
          eyes before giving a score.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Vivera® Retainers
        text: Vivera® retainers are a type of retainer that consists of clear plastic material and helps
          to maintain orthodontic corrections after a treatment.
