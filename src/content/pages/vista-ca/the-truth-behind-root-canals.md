    contentSections:
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/heading
      text: The Need for a Root Canal Procedure
      level: h2
      size: xl
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: The root canal is a crucial procedure for removing infected dental pulp from within a tooth.
        Beneath the outer shell of the tooth, known as the enamel, is the dentin and then the dental pulp.
        The dental pulp contains the blood vessels and nerves within each tooth. If the dental pulp becomes
        infected, then the patient will begin to experience incredible amounts of pain.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: Thus, a root canal procedure is necessary to remove the dental pulp and ensure the infection
        has not spread to other areas of the mouth. In fact, a regular dentist may not be enough to treat
        the root canal and the patient may need to see an endodontist. An endodontist is a dentist who
        specializes in maintaining teeth by treating the soft inner tissue of the teeth.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: People may even avoid seeking proper treatment due to believing these myths about root canals.
        There are several myths about root canals that people still believe. Below are the myths and the
        truths behind each of them.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/heading
      text: '#1. Root canals are painful'
      level: h3
      size: md
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: The truth is that root canals are not painful like people believe. Most professional endodontists
        will use anesthesia to numb the area of the procedure and help the patient relax. Thanks to the
        anesthesia, the patient will not feel any pain during the procedure and the endodontist can proceed
        without issue.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: Following the procedure, the patient may feel some level of soreness, but will be able to
        treat the soreness with pain killers or stronger medication the endodontist recommends.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/heading
      text: '#2. I will be unable to eat hard foods after a root canal procedure'
      level: h3
      size: md
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: Actually, a root canal will not prevent you from ever consuming your favorite foods again.
        With the root canal procedure, you will no longer feel pain since the procedure removes the nerve
        of the tooth. Once the endodontist covers the tooth back up, you will actually have less feeling
        within that tooth than before.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: Immediately following the procedure, there may be a few days in which you need to eat carefully
        and avoid biting directly on hard foods with the tooth. However, after this period, you will be
        able to eat what you want when you want. While a dental crown that covers the tooth may require
        maintenance from time to time, you will be able to continue eating and living on your regular
        schedule.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/heading
      text: '#3. Root canals require the dentist to remove the entire tooth'
      level: h3
      size: md
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: This is false. In reality, we want to keep the natural teeth in the best shape possible and
        only recommend a complete extraction when it is completely necessary. Thus, an endodontist will
        use a root canal to remove the infected portion of the tooth and cover the tooth back up to ensure
        that the infection does not spread.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: There is no reason to remove the entire tooth unless the infection consumes the tooth beyond
        repair and is spreading to other teeth. This is why we recommend that people seek treatment from
        an endodontist as soon as possible.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/heading
      text: '#4. Root canals cause illnesses in the patient'
      level: h3
      size: md
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: While some people may believe that root canals make you more susceptible to illnesses and
        disease, it is not true. There is no evidence that states this fact that professionals did not
        debunk. The truth is that you are more likely to get sick from leaving the infection in the tooth
        and not seeking a root canal procedure. If the infection is in an upper tooth and it spreads through
        the sinuses, a patient can die if he or she does not seek treatment.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: With proper dental hygiene and regular checkups, we can help to keep your teeth healthy. In
        the case of a serious dental infection, an endodontist can help effectively remove the infection
        without having to remove the entire tooth.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/heading
      text: '#5. Root canals will take multiple appointments'
      level: h3
      size: md
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: While an endodontist may need two appointments, one to identify that a root canal is necessary
        and one to perform the root canal, a root canal will not require multiple visits. If the patient
        schedules an emergency appointment due to unbearable pain, then the endodontist will do everything
        possible to treat the tooth immediately.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: If the patient needs more intensive endodontic surgery, then the endodontist will schedule
        a second appointment specifically for the surgery. It is understandable that people have busy
        schedules, but it will be extremely difficult to function each day with a dental infection.

  - _component: page-sections/builders/custom-section
    label: Definition of Endodontic Terminology
    contentSections:
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/heading
      text: Definition of Endodontic Terminology
      level: h3
      size: md
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list
      items:
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Cementum
        text: Cementum is that bone-like tissue that forms the outer surface on the root of the tooth.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Dental Pulp
        text: Dental pulp is the inner-most layer of the tooth with connective tissue that contains blood
          vessels and nerve tissue.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Dentin
        text: Dentin is the inner layer of the tooth structure that is immediately under the enamel and
          surrounds the dental pulp.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Direct Pulp Cap
        text: A direct pulp cap is a procedure in which a professional treats exposed pulp with a therapeutic
          material to help the tooth heal.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Enamel
        text: The enamel is the hard calcified layer that covers the entire tooth and is subject to interaction
          with multiple substances.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Endodontist
        text: An endodontist is a specialist who focuses on treating issues, diseases and conditions that
          affect the inner-most layer of the tooth, the dental pulp.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Pulpectomy
        text: A pulpectomy is a procedure that involves the complete removal of pulp tissue from the root
          canal in a tooth.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Pulpitis
        text: Pulpitis is another term to describe the inflammation of the dental pulp due to an injury
          or infection.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Pulpotomy
        text: A pulpotomy is a procedure involving the removal of a portion of diseased or infected pulp
          in order to protect the healthy portions of the pulp and teeth still in the mouth.
