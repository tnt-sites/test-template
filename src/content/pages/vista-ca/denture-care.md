      level: h2

    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: For a patient with removable dentures, it is crucial to remove them at night and thoroughly
        clean them. At least once a day, preferably at night, the individual needs to remove the dentures
        and carefully rinse off any loose food particles. However, the individual will not want to use
        typical toothpaste to clean the dentures.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: Regular toothpaste can actually damage the base of the dentures due to the abrasive particles
        in the toothpaste. There are specific denture cleaning fluids that one can purchase to use for
        cleaning the dentures. We can go over where to get these solutions during a consultation. It is
        also wise to get a specific brush to clean the dentures. Using a brittle toothbrush can actually
        do more damage than good to the dentures.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: When cleaning these dentures, it is important to keep the dentures above a towel or soft surface.
        Dropping the dentures, even just a few inches above a hard surface, can cause them to crack or
        break. Dentures must stay wet at all times and must not become dry. Thus, it is best to keep the
        dentures in water when one is not wearing them at night. In some cases, patients can keep their
        dentures in a cleaning solution overnight but will want to ask the dentist if that is best.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: By following these steps or the directions that a dentist gives, the patient will be able
        to continue wearing the dentures for long periods of time without damaging the dentures. We may
        recommend regular checkups to help keep an eye on the patient's oral health to ensure the dentures
        are in working order.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: If the dentures are not removable and stick to the patient's gums, then the dentist will recommend
        a different cleaning regimen. In many cases, caring for implant dentures will be the same as taking
        care of regular teeth with brushing twice a day. Patients need to take care of their gums and
        mouth as well.

    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: If a patient, who wears dentures, does not take care of his or her mouth, then there is a
        greater chance for infection to form; even when one cares for the dentures. For patients who can
        remove their dentures, they need to gently brush their gums, palate and tongue. Brushing the mouth
        without the dentures inside it will help to prevent plaque buildup and infection. It is also crucial
        to seek regular oral health exams from the dentist.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: Even if the patient has lost all of his or her teeth, it is important to seek professional
        dental care on a regular basis. A dentist will be able to tell a patient with dentures how often
        the patient needs to seek professional treatment. A dentist will be able to spot any damage and
        possible signs of infection before they spread.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
      text: If the patient needs any adjustments for the dentures due to a crack or chip in the dentures,
        we will repair or replace the dentures if necessary. In some cases, it will be easier to replace
        the dentures entirely. Dentures typically last about 5 to 10 years before needing replacement.
        Keeping the dentures in good condition can help prevent the need for more frequent visits to the
        dentist.

  - _component: page-sections/builders/custom-section
    label: Definition of Denture Terminology
    contentSections:
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/heading
      text: Definition of Denture Terminology
      level: h3
      size: md
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list
      items:
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Alveolar Bone
        text: The alveolar bone is the bone surrounding the root of the tooth that keeps the tooth in
          place.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Clasp
        text: A clasp is a device that holds a removable partial denture prosthesis to the teeth.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Denture Base
        text: The denture base is the part of the denture that connects the artificial teeth with the
          soft tissue of the gums.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Edentulous
        text: Edentulous is a term that applies to people who do not have any teeth.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Periodontal Disease
        text: Periodontal disease is a condition that causes inflammation of the gingival tissues and
          membrane of the teeth, leading to tooth loss without professional treatment.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Pontic
        text: Pontic is another term for an artificial tooth on a fixed partial denture.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Rebase
        text: Rebase is the process of refitting denture prosthesis by replacing the base material.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Reline
        text: Reline is when a professional resurfaces the surface of the prosthesis with a new base material.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Resin/Acrylic
        text: Resin and Acrylic are resinous materials that can be components in a denture base.
      - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
        title: Stomatitis
        text: Stomatitis is the inflammation of the tissue that is underlying a denture that does not
          fit properly. It can also result from other oral health factors.
