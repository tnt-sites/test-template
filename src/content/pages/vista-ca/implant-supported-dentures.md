  - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
    text: If you are looking for a durable and comfortable way to replace missing teeth, you should consider
      this solution. Implant supported dentures make it easier to eat your favorite meals and engage in
      normal daily activities since they will remain securely in your mouth. You will not need to worry

  - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/heading
    text: 'The Process of Getting Implant Supported Dentures: The Procedure'
    level: h2
    size: md
  - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
    text: Before we can begin the actual procedure, we will need to examine your gums to ensure you do
      not have any gum disease that needs to be addressed. If you do, we will treat it first. Next, we
      will take X-rays to determine the density of your jawbone and if it is sufficient for supporting
      the implants. Typically it will be, since these implants are shorter than traditional ones. We will
      then take an impression or mold of your mouth along with measurements. This information will be
      sent to the lab so they can create your new set of false teeth to our exact specifications.
  - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/text
    text: When it is time for the implant procedure, we will have you come back in and numb your gums
      and provide you with anesthesia. Typically, we will then place four small implants under your gum
      line. These implants will serve as anchors that your new set of teeth can attach to. Once they are
      firmly in place, we can snap in your dentures. These implants are an alternative to simply hoping
      your dentures stay in place or using adhesive to ensure they do. With implants to secure to, your
      teeth will not fall out, even if you are going on a run or eating pizza. For more information, call
      our Vista, CA dental office at 760-940-2273.

- _component: page-sections/builders/custom-section
  label: Definition of Denture Terminology
  contentSections:
  - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/heading
    text: Definition of Denture Terminology
    level: h3
    size: md
  - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list
    items:
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
      title: Alveolar Bone
      text: The alveolar bone is the bone surrounding the root of the tooth that keeps the tooth in place.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
      title: Clasp
      text: A clasp is a device that holds a removable partial denture prosthesis to the teeth.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
      title: Denture Base
      text: The denture base is the part of the denture that connects the artificial teeth with the soft
        tissue of the gums.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
      title: Edentulous
      text: Edentulous is a term that applies to people who do not have any teeth.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
      title: Periodontal Disease
      text: Periodontal disease is a condition that causes inflammation of the gingival tissues and membrane
        of the teeth, leading to tooth loss without professional treatment.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
      title: Pontic
      text: Pontic is another term for an artificial tooth on a fixed partial denture.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
      title: Rebase
      text: Rebase is the process of refitting denture prosthesis by replacing the base material.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
      title: Reline
      text: Reline is when a professional resurfaces the surface of the prosthesis with a new base material.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
      title: Resin/Acrylic
      text: Resin and Acrylic are resinous materials that can be components in a denture base.
    - _component: building-blocks/core-elements/definition-list/definition-list-item
      title: Stomatitis
      text: Stomatitis is the inflammation of the tissue that is underlying a denture that does not fit
        properly. It can also result from other oral health factors.
