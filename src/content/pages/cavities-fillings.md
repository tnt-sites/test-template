        html: Why Do Cavities Happen?
      - type: prose
        html: Cavities are incredibly common and can affect anyone, no matter how careful you are with your oral hygiene. Every day, a biofilm made up of acids and bacteria forms on your teeth. If this biofilm isn’t thoroughly removed by brushing and flossing, it hardens into plaque. Plaque buildup is often worse in hard-to-reach areas, like crowded teeth or deep grooves in molars. Over time, if not addressed, the bacteria can cause decay, leading to cavities.
      - type: prose
        html: Poor oral hygiene can also cause gum inflammation, known as gingivitis, which can progress into periodontal disease if untreated. This advanced stage may lead to abscesses, tooth loss, and even jawbone deterioration. In severe cases, the infection can spread to the bloodstream, potentially causing dangerous conditions like sepsis.
      - type: prose
        html: However, even with good oral care habits, cavities can still occur. Brushing for less than two minutes, using a toothbrush that’s too hard, or brushing too soon after consuming acidic foods and drinks can contribute to enamel erosion. Additionally, certain medications that cause dry mouth can increase your risk of cavities by reducing the amount of saliva needed to wash away bacteria and acids. Without proper saliva flow, cavities can develop rapidly, sometimes within just six months.
