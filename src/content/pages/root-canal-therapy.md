      - type: heading
        html: What’s a root canal?
      - type: prose
        html: Many Sandy, Utah area patients who are told they could benefit from a root canal may be unsure what it is. Also known as endodontic therapy, the root canal procedure is performed to address a problem such as internal infection of the tooth, an extensive cavity, or an abscess. During the process, the dentist will create an access point into the inner canals of the tooth. Through this area, the dentist will then remove the dental pulp inside the tooth and disinfect the canals before filling the tooth. In some cases, the dentist may also create and bond a dental crown over the tooth to offer another layer of strength.

        html: Root Canal FAQs
      - type: subheading
        html: What Should I Do Before a Root Canal?
      - type: prose
        html: |-
          <p>Before you get a root canal, you can take several steps to prepare for it. Doing so will ensure your final
                      treatment is smooth and effective.</p>
          <p>For starters, please avoid alcohol and tobacco prior to your care. These substances can interfere with
                      numbing medication, making your root canal more irritating than necessary. &nbsp;You should instead just
                      have a healthy meal beforehand; it’ll keep you from feeling hungry during your procedure.</p>
          <p>Another tip is to take an over-the-counter pain reliever before your visit. That way, you can reduce your
                      potential discomfort after the numbing medication wears off.</p>
      - type: subheading
        html: How Long Does a Root Canal Take?
      - type: prose
        html: |-
          <p>A standard root canal only requires one dental visit, which can last anywhere from 30 to 90 minutes. However,
                      the length of your own treatment will depend on your personal circumstances.</p>
          <p>Imagine that you have a severe tooth infection, for example. Your dentist may have to divide your root canal
                      into two visits in this case. The first visit would place a temporary antibacterial medicine in the tooth,
                      while the second appointment would clean out your tooth’s innards.</p>
          <p>Alternatively, say it’s a molar that needs a root canal. This type of tooth has more roots than other
                      kinds, so disinfecting it will take longer. That means the final root canal will involve a greater amount of
                      time.</p>
      - type: subheading
        html: How Much Pain is Normal After a Root Canal?
      - type: prose
        html: |-
          <p>A root canal itself shouldn’t cause you pain, but you may feel some soreness after it’s over.
                      That’s a normal reaction to have once the numbing medication wears off.</p>
          <p>Of course, such post-treatment discomfort is temporary. It won’t (or at least shouldn’t) last
                      longer than three days at most. You can also manage it in the meantime with over-the-counter pain relievers,
                      especially those like acetaminophen and ibuprofen.</p>
          <p>Please call us quickly if your pain doesn’t fade after the three-day mark. In that case, you may have
                      some treatment complications that we should address.</p>
      - type: subheading
        html: Do I Need Antibiotics Before or After My Root Canal?
      - type: prose
        html: |-
          <p>As luck would have it, most patients don’t need antibiotics before or after a root canal. The treatment
                      is (often) safe enough that medicines aren’t required for it.</p>
          <p>A patient only needs antibiotics before a root canal when they have prior health issues. Even then, a dentist
                      will only prescribe the medicine when the problem increases your risk of an oral infection. You likely
                      won’t need an antibiotic otherwise; you can just directly proceed with treatment.</p>
          <p>That said, a dentist will review your medical history to see if you need an antibiotic. Those details will
                      show whether the medicine would benefit your root canal.</p>
